01

Overview & context

Nigerian banks lean on A2P SMS for transaction alerts — a channel that is costly at scale, character-limited, one-way, and increasingly ignored. WhatsApp reaches the same customers on a channel they already keep open, supports rich media and two-way replies, and — used correctly — can reshape both the cost line and the engagement story.

Octave Chime is a notification platform that sits beside a bank's core, listens for posted transactions, and pushes formatted alerts to WhatsApp. Two capabilities distinguish it from a plain SMS-to-WhatsApp swap: multi-recipient delivery (one transaction can alert several people — a joint-account pair, a business owner and their accountant) and a scheduled end-of-day summary that closes each account's day with opening balance, movement, and closing balance.

To demonstrate the platform without touching a live core banking system, we will build a lightweight simulation banking app. It performs realistic transactions — send money, check balance, pay bills, buy airtime — and every action emits a transaction event that flows through Chime to WhatsApp. The simulator is the demo vehicle; the notification platform is the product.

In one lineBuild a simulated bank that generates real transaction events, and a WhatsApp notification engine that turns each event into a multi-recipient alert plus a daily account summary.
02

Problem statement

  • SMS is expensive and getting more so. High-volume transaction alerting is a real cost line, and the pricing dynamics keep shifting under banks.
  • SMS is thin. 160 characters, no branding, no media, no reply path — a poor surface for anything beyond a bare alert, and useless for cross-sell.
  • Alerts are single-recipient by default. Joint accounts, corporate accounts with several signatories, and dependants monitored by a guardian all need the same alert to reach several people. SMS treats one account as one phone number.
  • No daily close. Customers get a stream of individual alerts but never a clean end-of-day picture — the single most useful artefact for trust and for spotting anomalies.
  • Fraud signal is weak. A one-way SMS gives the customer no fast, in-channel way to flag "not me."

Solution & architecture

The transaction is the event. Everything downstream reacts to it. Keeping the notification engine event-driven and decoupled from the "bank" means the same engine that listens to our simulator can later listen to a real core with no change to its logic.

SOURCESimulation BankSend money, bills, airtime, balance. Posts transactions to the ledger.
CORELedger ServiceDouble-entry balances, opening/closing, per-account day rollup.
BUSEvent Streamtransaction.posted events, idempotent, ordered per account.
BRAINNotification OrchestratorResolves recipients, picks template, renders, dispatches.
GATEWAYWhatsApp GatewayMeta Cloud API via BSP. Templates, delivery receipts, replies.
SUPPORTINGRecipient / Subscription ServiceAccount → recipients map, roles, consent, preferences.
SUPPORTINGEOD Summary EngineScheduled job; aggregates the day per account; sends summary.
SUPPORTINGTemplate ManagerApproved WhatsApp templates by category (utility / marketing).
SUPPORTINGAdmin & AnalyticsConsole + delivery, read, cost dashboards.
Design principleThe notification engine never talks to the bank directly — only to the event stream. Swap the simulator for a real core and nothing downstream changes.
07

Functional requirements

7.1Simulation banking app

  • Auth & onboarding: sign in, create demo customers, set WhatsApp number(s) & opt-in.
  • Account types: individual, joint, and corporate — the types that make multi-recipient meaningful.
  • Send money: intra-bank & simulated inter-bank transfer; debits sender, credits beneficiary (if internal).
  • Check balance: available & ledger balance.
  • Bill payment: utilities/TV/etc. from a demo biller list — debit.
  • Airtime / data: quick top-up — debit.
  • Mini-statement: last N transactions.
  • Every transaction emits a transaction.posted event — the trigger for all notifications.

7.2Real-time transaction notification

  • On each posted transaction, deliver a WhatsApp alert within the latency target.
  • Debit and credit variants, with amount, party, reference, timestamp, and available balance after.
  • Include an in-message fraud path ("Not you? Reply REPORT").
  • Idempotent: a retried event never double-sends.

7.3Multi-recipient delivery

  • Each account holds a list of recipients, each with a WhatsApp number, a role (owner, joint-holder, signatory, accountant, guardian, viewer), and per-recipient consent and preferences (which alert types; thresholds; EOD yes/no).
  • One transaction fans out to all eligible recipients as separate delivered messages.
  • Recipients can be added/removed in the admin console; consent is required before any send.
  • Threshold rules: e.g. accountant only alerted above ₦100k — configurable per recipient.

7.4End-of-day summary

  • Scheduled at configurable close-of-business (per bank timezone).
  • Per account, compute and send: opening balance, transaction count, transaction volume, total inflow, total outflow, net value, closing balance (definitions in §9).
  • Sent to all recipients who opted into the summary.
  • Idempotent and re-runnable for a given business date.

7.5WhatsApp integration

  • Meta WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) through a Business Solution Provider.
  • Business-initiated alerts use pre-approved utility templates; cross-sell uses marketing templates with separate opt-in.
  • Handle delivery/read receipts and inbound replies (for the fraud path & two-way CVM).
  • Respect the 24-hour service window; fall back to SMS (stub) when a number is not on WhatsApp.

7.6Admin & CVM console

  • Manage accounts, recipients, roles, consent, templates, and schedules.
  • Delivery, read, and cost dashboards; failed-message queue.
  • Hooks for cross-sell/upsell campaigns (design only in MVP).

Metric definitions

The concept listed count, volume, value, inflow, outflow, and balances. "Volume" and "value" get used loosely in Nigerian banking, so we pin exact definitions here — this is the one place ambiguity would cost us at build time.

FieldDefinitionFormula
Opening balanceAvailable balance carried from the prior business day's close= prior day Closing
Transaction countNumber of posted transactions in the day (debits + credits)= n(debits) + n(credits)
Transaction volumeGross monetary throughput — total money moved, regardless of direction= Σ|amount|
Total inflowSum of all credits= Σ credits
Total outflowSum of all debits= Σ debits
Net value "total value"Net movement across the day= Inflow − Outflow
Closing balanceAvailable balance at close of business= Opening + Inflow − Outflow
reconciliation identity
Closing = Opening + Inflow − Outflow
Net     = Inflow − Outflow = Closing − Opening
Volume  = Inflow + Outflow  // gross, ≠ Net
Count   = n(debits) + n(credits)
Decision neededConfirm the "volume vs value" reading. This spec sets Volume = gross money moved (Σ|amount|) and Value = net (Inflow − Outflow). If your convention is Volume = transaction count, we rename fields accordingly — but count and volume should not be the same number in the summary.

Non-functional requirements

Security & privacy

  • TLS in transit; encryption at rest for all customer & message data
  • Minimise PII inside templates; mask account numbers (*4821)
  • Consent required & auditable before any send (NDPA 2023)
  • Role-based access to the admin console; full audit log
  • Align with CBN e-banking guidance & WhatsApp Business policy

Reliability

  • At-least-once delivery with idempotency keys (no double alerts)
  • Retry with backoff; dead-letter queue for failures
  • EOD job re-runnable per business date without duplication

Performance & scale

  • Alert latency < 10 s p95 from posting to delivery
  • Handle burst throughput (e.g. salary-day spikes)
  • Horizontal scaling of orchestrator & gateway workers

Observability

  • Delivery, read, cost, and error metrics per template/account
  • Alerting on delivery-rate drops or gateway errors
  • Cost tracking per message for the §12 business case

Cost & business case

The concept slide assumes WhatsApp's old per-conversation pricing, where a 24-hour window covered many messages for one fixed fee. That model no longer exists. Meta moved to per-delivered-message pricing in July 2025, and from 1 October 2026 even in-window service replies become chargeable. This changes where the savings come from.

≈₦14
Reported cost of one utility (transaction) message to a Nigerian number under current per-message pricing (~$0.0101)
≈₦84
Cost of a marketing message (~$0.062) — cross-sell is a different, pricier category
Tiers
Volume tiers reduce utility rates as monthly volume grows
Honest read on the 50% claimUnder per-message pricing, a single WhatsApp utility alert is not automatically cheaper than a single SMS, and multi-recipient multiplies message count. So "50% cheaper" cannot be assumed — it must be modelled. The real savings levers are: (1) consolidation — the EOD summary replaces a cluster of individual SMS; (2) volume tiers at bank scale; (3) free inbound-triggered utility messages inside the service window; and (4) value, not just cost — fraud reduction and cross-sell revenue that SMS can't deliver. Action: build a side-by-side cost model with the client's real SMS rate card and alert volumes before we commit to a savings figure.

Figures above are recent public reports of Meta's Nigeria rate card and move with the naira; treat them as directional and confirm against Meta's live pricing before modelling.

Design direction

The simulator has to look like a bank people would trust and a product Octave would ship — because in a pitch, the demo is the argument. It inherits the Octave system this document is built in.

Visual language

  • Palette: near-black #0A0F12, teal #16C0A8, gold #E7B24B, crimson #E5484D for debits/alerts
  • Type: Fraunces (balances & display), DM Sans (UI), JetBrains Mono (amounts, refs)
  • Feel: confident dark fintech surface, generous space, one bold moment per screen — the balance

Sandbox screens

  • Home / balance with quick actions (Send · Bills · Airtime · Statement)
  • Send money flow → instant on-screen confirmation
  • Live "notifications" panel mirroring what hits WhatsApp
  • Recipients screen (add owner / accountant / signatory)
  • Operator view to fire transactions during a demo

If you'd rather I produce static high-fidelity mockups first, I can do that too.

Open questions

  1. Volume vs value — confirm the definitions in §9 (this is the one decision that shapes the summary schema).
  2. Multi-recipient consent — does each recipient opt in individually, or does the account owner authorise on their behalf? Regulatory implications differ.
  3. Close-of-business time — fixed (e.g. 18:00) or per-account configurable?
  4. Fallback — is SMS fallback in MVP scope, or stubbed for the demo?
  5. Target client — is this pitched generically or tailored to a named bank's brand for the demo?
  6. Sandbox reach — do alerts go to your real WhatsApp during demos (needs a live BSP/number), or is a simulated WhatsApp panel enough for the pitch?
